## Where the money actually goes

Most "how to buy" pages stop at "low fees" and move on. Your cost has three separate layers, and only one of them is the trading fee people argue about online.

**Platform trading fee.** OKX doesn't publish a rate on the buy page. The number that applies to your order appears before you confirm it. Check it there. Anyone quoting you a fixed percentage without saying whether it's maker or taker, spot or futures, is guessing at your situation.

**Payment channel cost.** This is the one buyers forget. A card purchase adds a processing fee on top. A bank transfer trades speed for a lower cost. If you're moving a meaningful amount, the 1–3 day wait is often the cheapest hour of your week.

**Exit costs.** Withdrawal fees depend on the coin and the network, and they're shown on the withdrawal screen. Then there's tax, which is a cost in the practical sense even though it isn't charged by anyone on the exchange.

Security on the exchange side only covers half the problem. The other half is where you keep the ETH once you own it.

- **Custodial wallet:** a service holds the keys. Convenient, and you're relying on that service.
- **Non-custodial wallet:** you hold the keys. You're also the only backstop if you lose them.
- **Hot wallet:** connected to the internet. Fine for amounts you plan to move soon.
- **Cold wallet:** offline storage. More friction, better suited to holdings you don't intend to touch.

Whichever you pick, back up the wallet and keep the recovery phrase somewhere that isn't a screenshot in your photo library. A recovery phrase is the asset. Anyone who reads it owns your ETH.

## Selling ETH back into dollars

The exit is less obvious than the entry, and it's where new buyers get stuck.

You place a sell order on an exchange that supports fiat withdrawals. Depending on the platform, you may be able to sell ETH straight into your local currency, or you may need to convert it into a widely traded pair like BTC or USDT first and cash that out. After the sale, the balance shows up as fiat in your account, and you withdraw through whatever channels are open to you: bank transfer, card withdrawal, or a third-party payment provider.

Most exchanges require full account verification before enabling fiat withdrawals, especially at larger amounts. Confirm your details are current before you need the money, not on the day you need it. Withdrawal fees, limits and processing times vary by channel and by platform, so check all three before you sell.

## Taxes, in the US framing

Buying ETH is generally not a taxable event by itself. Selling or swapping it is, and any gain over your cost basis is reportable as capital gains. Staking rewards and similar income may also be taxable when received.

Rules differ by jurisdiction and they change. The specific treatment of your situation is a question for a tax professional, not for an exchange help page.
